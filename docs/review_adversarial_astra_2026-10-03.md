## 1. Strongest attack: the batch avoids the experiment most likely to
invalidate its model

You acknowledge that the model cannot rank the promising region, then allocate
two slots to exploiting it and another to exploring uncertainty generated by
that same unreliable model.

The evidence is unusually damaging:

• Within round 3, GP leave-one-out Spearman is 0.086 (reports/tables/
  model_loo_ranking_by_round.csv:6).

• Slots 1 and 4 save only €0.74/L and €0.12/L, respectively, against E19.

• Removing E14 shifts the exploration recipe by 40% of volume; changing the
  kernel’s coordinates shifts slot 4 by 72% (batch_stability.csv).

• Yet E10/E14—the nearby recipes differing by 40.63 viability points—are not
  rerun.

A defensible competing batch is E19 + E10 + E14 + current slots 1 and 2. That
is five formulations, retaining a local improvement and a cheaper alternative
while replacing speculative exploration/EI with a diagnostic comparison.

E10 and E14 cost €176.04/L and €176.35/L, both below E19’s €178.43/L; their
historical viabilities are 48.15% and 7.52% (data/processed/
pbmc_analysis.csv). Concurrent reruns would test whether their discrepancy
reproduces under shared conditions. They would not establish its historical
cause, but they could change the next modelling decision far more than another
blend near 44% DMEM.

This is not a proven optimal batch either. Its advantage is a clear decision
question. The submission must explain why model-dependent variance reduction
is worth more than resolving this contradiction. The brief permits 3–5
formulations; it does not require every formulation to be new.

## 2. Internal inconsistencies and claims exceeding the evidence

“No policy differs from random” is not the result.
reports/memo.md:73; reports/model_comparison.md:105: nonsignificant tests with
30 seeds do not establish equivalence. Moreover, the sign tests concern
discovery regret, not finalist regret (scripts/compare_models.py:367–379). On
the low-noise GP truth, UCB wins discovery in 22/30 seeds but wins finalist
selection in only 13/30; median finalist regret is 2.66 versus random’s 2.12.
Say: “We did not detect a discovery advantage at high noise; finalist
performance remains uncertain.” Label the 16 policy-setting tests exploratory;
the unadjusted p=0.016 is not strong family-wide evidence.

“Confirmation limits the outcome more than candidate choice” is an untested
intervention claim.
memo.md:73; model_comparison.md:106. The simulator selects the largest noisy
observation (compare_models.py:281), implements no confirmation, and allocates
no budget between replication and discovery. It demonstrates a selection-error
problem under that decision rule. It does not establish the best remedy or
budget allocation.

“The rules themselves … did most of the work” lacks a controlled ablation.
walkthrough.md:43; model_comparison.md:103. The comparison with earlier regret
7.82 changes the candidate universe and therefore its optimum and regret
scale, alongside eligibility rules and simulation changes. It cannot isolate
the benefit of the rules.

The pilot cannot estimate the variance promised for confirmation.
memo.md:81,101; walkthrough.md:67,72: one shared cell preparation and one
preparation of each medium provide no independent replication of preparation
effects. Four wells measure within-preparation variation. The ±13-point
interval and 77%/23% pass rates are conditional calculations using historical
SD 9.51, not validated operating characteristics for the changed assay
protocol. lab_plan.md:40 partly repairs this by mentioning repeats; the memo
and answers still imply this plate suffices.

Coordinate sensitivity is a prior-sensitivity result, not information lost by
deleting DMEM.
memo.md:83: three fractions determine the fourth. Dropping DMEM while
retaining a diagonal ARD kernel changes the allowed covariance geometry. A 72%
recommendation shift warrants explaining the chosen prior, not merely
“flagging rather than resolving” it.

A screening pass becomes an unwarranted biological conclusion.
memo.md:98: “RPMI-10 can stand in for the serum-free media” follows a screen
that lets a blend 10 points worse pass 23% of the time. Replace with:
“Prioritize this substitution for independent confirmation.”

## 4. Five concepts the candidate must explain himself

1. Experimental unit: Four wells from one preparation cannot tell me how
   results vary between independent preparations.

2. GP uncertainty: A narrow posterior can reflect strong, wrong assumptions
   rather than reliable knowledge.

3. Mixture geometry: Removing a redundant fraction preserves recipe
   information but can change the smoothness assumptions imposed by the
   kernel.

4. Batch acquisition: Pretending pending results equal their predictions
   reduces conditional uncertainty; it does not create evidence or prove those
   picks are best.

5. Decision statistics: Failing to detect a difference does not prove
   equivalence, and passing a noisy screen does not establish non-inferiority.
