Next media batch: test the DMEM signal at E19's cost

The full analysis in pictures. Every section shows the evidence first, then the wording a reviewer reads in the memo, then what it means in plain words. Every number on this page is read from the repository's outputs.

Code and documents: github.com/harshpuri84/qorium-media-optimisation · memo: reports/memo.pdf (4 pages) · I am not a cell biologist; AI assistants wrote code and drafts and reviewed the work, and I set the questions and made the calls.

01 · The decision

The answer

The data cannot tell us which blend is best. They point to one signal, DMEM near 44%, and a model more confident about it than its forward test supports. The next plate tests that signal directly, at about E19's cost, with a contrast fixed before the run that may come back inconclusive.

The five formulations

Bars show the blend of the four media. On the right: the model's estimate with its 80% predictive interval for one measured mean. E19's estimate is 70.1, shrunk from the 81.0 it scored.

DMEMRPMI-10X-VIVO 15AR5
Memo wording

Five formulations, 11 randomised wells each: two designed DMEM steps around the best blend, two model picks, and a same-plate re-run of the best historical blend (E19).

Slots 1 and 2 test the model's belief in a sharp DMEM peak on one line through E19. Slot 3 asks whether RPMI-10 can replace both serum-free media; slot 4 whether X-VIVO 15 can replace AR5, the medium with no public price.

In plain words

Think of it as a pilot on four lanes plus a control lane. Two lanes test the one thing the data hint at (how much DMEM). One lane tests a cheaper mix. One tests dropping the ingredient we cannot price. The control lane is our best blend run again on the same day, so we compare like with like.

We run each lane 11 times because single results are noisy: about 10 points either way.

02 · The decision

The method in one picture

Nine steps from a public spreadsheet to a plate a scientist can run. Click a step to jump to it.

Memo wording

The GP proposes candidates and the plate decides. Designed contrasts test the model's sharpest assumption; model picks cover cost and the most promising change. A validity gate, a non-inferiority screen and an independent confirmation turn plate results into a decision.

In plain words

Clean the data, price the ingredients, check how much the model can be trusted, pick candidates, stress-test the picks, then plan the experiment so its result is readable. The model is a forecaster that has been wrong before, so the plan does not depend on it being right.

03 · The problem

The brief and Qorium

The brief

A lab wants better cell growth at lower media cost. Experiments are slow, noisy and run 3 to 5 at a time. Recommend the next 3 to 5 formulations, balancing performance, cost, uncertainty, assay noise, feasibility and batch size; compare at least two approaches; explain how the strategy changes in six scenarios.

It is an experiment-recommendation problem, not a prediction contest.

Qorium

Maastricht company growing leather from cow skin cells (fibroblasts) in bioreactors. They take a small biopsy from a live cow, grow the cells into collagen sheets, and tan them like hide. The process uses no animal products beyond the cells. Raised EUR 22M in November 2025. Co-founder and CSO: Prof. Mark Post, who made the first cultured beef burger in 2013.

Media is one of the biggest running costs at production scale, which is why this case exists.

Sources: qorium.com; Pulse 2.0.

Memo wording

DMEM share is the only blend variable with a clear signal. It stands for what differs between DMEM and RPMI: glucose, calcium, bicarbonate (pH) and osmolality, which this design cannot separate. Across rounds 1 and 2, blends at 29 to 33% DMEM scored 48 to 49%, E12 at 44.5% scored 68.1%, and blends at 60 to 77% scored 43 to 56%. All six round-3 blends sit at 44 to 45% DMEM and score 67 to 81% while their other three components vary widely.

In plain words

One dial seems to matter: how much DMEM is in the mix. Around 44% works; much less or much more does worse. The other three ingredients swing a lot in the best round without changing the result much.

The catch: all the good results came on the same "day" (round 3). That could be a good recipe or a good day. The paper re-ran the best recipe in a control comparison and got 80%. Assuming that was a separate day, it is probably mostly the recipe.

06 · The evidence

Prices and the cost rule

Price per litre of prepared medium

Supplier list prices, 2 October 2026. DMEM and RPMI-10 include 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics, as in the paper. AR5 has no public price and is set equal to X-VIVO 15.

Base mediumFBS shareAntibioticsComplete serum-free medium
Memo wording

Model picks cost no more per litre than E19 in at least 6 of 9 FBS and AR5 price scenarios. The designed steps may cost up to 1% more: 50% DMEM is EUR 0.26/L over E19, a gap smaller than the uncertainty in the FBS price itself. Savings against E19 are small (up to 2.53%), so this ceiling screens candidates; it is not a business case.

In plain words

All four media cost about the same once serum is added, EUR 168 to 181 per litre. Serum is three quarters of the cost of the two classic media. So the rule is simple: do not pay more than for our best blend. The real business test, cost per unit of good output, needs Qorium's own numbers.

The serum price came from a search snippet and AR5 has no price at all, so I tested nine price scenarios.

07 · The evidence

How far to trust the model

The forward test

Train on earlier rounds, predict the next round's average. Open dot: the GP's prediction. Filled dot: what happened.

Ranking skill lives between rounds, not within them

Leave-one-out rank correlation between predicted and observed viability, GP with pooled noise. 1 is perfect ranking, 0 is chance.

Memo wording

On all 24 blends no model beats the mean on predictive density (NLPD, lower is better; 4.44 for the mean against 5.93 for the GP). The GP's leave-one-out rank correlation of 0.61 comes from separating round 3 from the rest; within rounds 0 to 2 it is 0.11. Every model under-predicted round 3 by 22 to 29 points. So the GP proposes candidates and the plate decides.

The model pulls E19's observed 81.0 down to 70.1, because it combines E19's readings with its lower-scoring neighbours and the fitted noise. Its DMEM length scale sits at its floor: it believes viability changes sharply with DMEM.

In plain words

The model (a Gaussian process, GP) is a forecaster that also says how unsure it is. Tested honestly, it was wrong by 27 points on the round that mattered. It can tell "round-3 style" from "earlier style", but not which round-3 blend is best.

So I use it to suggest where to look and to show where it is overconfident, not to promise results.

08 · The evidence

How Bayesian optimisation picks experiments

A toy with one ingredient so the logic is visible. Click anywhere to run an experiment there, or let the model pick. Made-up curve, not our data.

Key ideas
  • The band is the model's uncertainty. It shrinks where you have tested.
  • κ = 0 only exploits and can get stuck next to a mediocre peak.
  • Batches: pick one, pretend its result equals the forecast, pick again. The pretend result narrows the band there, so the next pick goes elsewhere. That is pending-point conditioning.
In plain words

Choosing pilot lanes when each pilot is expensive: try lanes the forecast likes, and some it is unsure about, because one test in a blind spot can change every later decision.

09 · The batch

Why these four, and what was rejected

The batch against the history

Historical blends on the two components the model responds to, coloured by viability. Stars are the picks; the ring is E19. The dashed line is the DMEM titration through E19.

How close each candidate is to E19, in the model's eyes

Correlation with E19 under the model. Near 1 means the model already thinks it knows the answer: a well spent there teaches little.

Memo wording

An earlier version used four model picks. One of them, 44/26/19/11, was 0.981 correlated with E19 under the model and predicted at 70.2 against E19's 70.1: a well the model priced at nothing and the assay could not resolve. Independent reviewers flagged it, and I replaced it and the exploration pick with the two DMEM steps.

A reviewer also proposed re-running E10 and E14. I rejected it: the two ran in different rounds, so the gap most plausibly reflects donor or preparation, and re-running both on one plate tests that preparation, not the medium.

In plain words

If the forecaster already rates a lane the same as the control lane, piloting it tells you almost nothing. Better to pilot where the forecaster is confident but nobody has checked: 40% and 50% DMEM.

This is the clearest place where I overrode the model's own pick, and one place where I overrode a reviewer.

10 · The batch

What could move the picks

Sensitivity heatmap

Each row reruns the selection with one change. Each cell is how far (in % of volume) that slot's pick moved. The designed steps never move, so only the model slots vary.

Memo wording

The DMEM steps are fixed by design. Slot 3 moved by a median 16% of volume, mainly with prices; slot 4 by a median 6%, and by 72% only in the fit without DMEM as a coordinate. Four fractions summing to one carry one redundant coordinate; dropping DMEM hides the signal inside the other three. I keep all four with a floor on the length scales; log-ratio coordinates (once zero components are handled) or orthonormal simplex coordinates are the options for the next round.

In plain words

I changed one assumption at a time and watched whether the picks moved. The cheap pick moves when serum prices move, which is what a cost pick should do. The AR5 swap holds in almost every test.

11 · The batch

The simulation

How far below the best does the final pick land?

30 paired campaigns of 18 blends on two made-up "true" response surfaces, same rules and same measurement errors for every policy. The lab picks the blend with the highest noisy result; the dot is how far that is below the best available. Filled: low noise. Open: realistic noise (11.8 points).

Memo wording

Random picks inside the rules ended 1.6 to 2.1 points from the best blend. Only UCB showed nominal evidence of beating random, at low noise (sign test p = 0.016 and 0.001; across 16 exploratory tests only 0.001 clears a Bonferroni threshold of 0.003). At the fitted noise of 11.8 points I detected no advantage for any policy, and the blend picked from single noisy readings sat 4.7 to 7.9 points below the best. That is why this plate spends wells on replicates and two slots on designed contrasts rather than four model picks.

In plain words

I played the experiment 30 times on fake data where I know the right answer. With this much noise, clever picking barely beats random picking. What hurts is choosing the winner from one noisy result. So the plate measures each blend 11 times instead of chasing more candidates.

12 · The batch

The plate and the decision

The 96-well plate

Rows are blocks: each formulation appears twice in five rows and once in the sixth, at random positions. The edge ring holds PBS so the inner wells do not dry out. Click a formulation to highlight its wells.

STEP 1
Is the plate valid?

Blanks, heat-killed controls, missing wells and spread are checked against rules set before the run. Invalid plates are repeated, not interpreted.

STEP 2
Read the DMEM steps

Pre-registered test: E19 minus the mean of the 40% and 50% steps. Predicted 5.6 points, SE about 3.5, power about 48%. "Inconclusive" is allowed. Read regardless of cost.

STEP 3
Screen slots 3 and 4

A blend passes if its mean is no more than 5 points below the same-plate E19, at equal or lower cost. A pass means "not excluded".

STEP 4
Confirm, then decide

One finalist against E19 on independent cell and medium preparations, on different days. Accept if the lower bound stays above minus 5 points.

How sharp is the screen?

Chance a blend passes the 5-point screen, by its true difference from E19. Move the slider to see what replicates buy.

Memo wording

With 11 wells per arm the 90% interval on the difference is about plus or minus 7 points: a blend equal to E19 passes 89% of the time, one 10 points worse 11%. A lead needs a mean above E19 by more than the interval. This plate estimates variation within one preparation only; independent repeats come first and size the confirmation.

In plain words

The two DMEM steps are a question, not candidates: does viability drop on both sides of 44%? The plate has about even odds of seeing that, so "can't tell" is a fair answer and I say so up front. The other two are a filter, not a verdict. It throws out clear losers. Anything that survives gets a proper trial on fresh cells and fresh media on different days, because all 55 wells on this plate share one batch of cells, so they cannot tell you how much results move between batches.

13 · Beyond the case

What changes for Qorium

PBMC case studyQorium version
Suspension immune cells, viability at 72 hAdherent bovine fibroblasts, growth to confluence and collagen over weeks. Dead cells detach, so viability says little; growth rate and collagen are the endpoints
Two media carry 10% FBSAnimal-free list: no FBS, no animal-derived insulin, transferrin, albumin or trypsin. Human dermal fibroblasts grow well serum-free; for bovine skin fibroblasts that is an expectation to test, and the published gap is for bovine myoblasts (Kolkmann, Post et al. 2020). Two media are needed, expansion and collagen production (ascorbate, 2 to 4 weeks), plus animal-free attachment and dissociation reagents
Four complete media on a simplex8 to 15 defined components in a box; needs priors or a screening design first
Cost per litre against E19Medium cost per gram of acceptable collagen or per square metre of sheet
A round takes daysA round takes weeks, so each round must carry more information
What carries over, what changes

Carries over: anchor every round, pending-point conditioning, designed contrasts where the model is overconfident, validity gate, screen then confirm, cost as a constraint across price scenarios.

Changes: candidate generator (box, not simplex), kernel inputs and priors, endpoints and their noise, cost denominator, decision rules.

Questions for the CSO
  • Which endpoint decides a medium change: growth, collagen per cell, sheet properties or cost per square metre?
  • What loss is acceptable for what saving, and who signs it off?
  • Which ingredients are already ruled in or out?
  • How well do plate results predict the bioreactor?
  • How much do cell batches and biopsies vary, and is it recorded?

Most important: the simulation did not give every policy the same noise. Fixed, and the result reversed: at realistic noise no policy beat random. Also added plate controls, viable cell counts, a licence and the 3-coordinate checks.

Two reviewers told to break it

Biologist and data-scientist reviews of the final memo

DMEM share is a proxy for glucose, calcium, pH and osmolality. The steps cannot "settle" the peak (about 48% power), so the curvature test is pre-registered with "inconclusive" allowed. Added t = 0 read, two media phases for Qorium, and fixed seven mismatches.

How I describe it

I am not a microbiologist and I do not present this as my research. What I want to show is that I can understand a scientific problem well enough to frame the right question, direct AI to build and attack the solution, and make the calls: which findings to accept, which to reject, and what to recommend.

Calls I made myself
  • Chose the public PBMC data over the primary paper's unlabelled arrays.
  • Chose a cost constraint over a weighted score, because a lab manager can check it.
  • Picked the DMEM titration over the reviewer's E10/E14 re-run.
  • Spent idle wells on replicates instead of more formulations.
  • Kept E14 in the fit and stated why.

15 · Beyond the case

Interview prep

Seven ideas I must explain in my own words

IdeaOne sentence
ConfoundingAll the good recipes ran on one day, so "good recipe" and "good day" cannot be separated without re-running a recipe on another day; the anchor does that.
ShrinkageWith 12 points of noise, the model pulls E19's 81 down to 70, because the best of several noisy results is partly luck.
Length scale at a boundAt the floor, the model believes viability changes sharply with DMEM; at the ceiling, it sees no effect of RPMI-10 or AR5 and treats them as interchangeable.
Pending-point conditioningPretending a pick already returned its forecast narrows uncertainty there so the next pick goes elsewhere; it changes the uncertainty, never the forecast.
Experimental unitEleven wells from one cell preparation tell you about that preparation; how results vary between preparations needs independent repeats.
Mixture geometryFour fractions that sum to 100% have only three free directions, so which three the model sees changes its assumptions.
Non-inferiorityFailing to find a difference does not prove two blends are equal; "not excluded" is the most a screen can say.

Likely questions

Why trust a GP that missed round 3 by 27 points?

I don't, as a forecast. I use it to propose candidates and to show where it is overconfident. The DMEM steps test that overconfidence directly.

Why designed points instead of the model's picks?

The simulation found no reliable advantage for any policy at this noise level, and the model's sharpest assumption is untested. A designed contrast answers a clear question whatever the model believes.

What does P(above E19) = 0.39 mean?

Under the model, a 39% chance slot 4's true viability beats E19's. It ignores batch effects, which is why E19 runs on the same plate.

Why 11 wells?

The plate had 36 idle interior wells. Eleven per arm cut the interval on each comparison from about 13 to about 7 points without adding formulations.

Why is 50% DMEM allowed above the cost ceiling?

It is EUR 0.26/L over, smaller than the uncertainty in the FBS price. Designed points may be 1% over; model picks must stay under.

Does a PBMC blend tell you anything about bovine fibroblasts?

Only the method. Fibroblasts attach to surfaces and need animal-free attachment and dissociation reagents. Human dermal fibroblasts grow well serum-free; for bovine skin fibroblasts that is an expectation to test. Your 2020 work showed the gap for bovine myoblasts, and collagen needs ascorbate and 2 to 4 weeks, so it is a separate production medium.

How do you know 81% is real and not debris?

I don't. The plate adds heat-killed controls to set the dead-cell gate and blanks for background, and records viable cells per mL as well as viability.

How would you build the team around this loop?

Start with a modeller-engineer and a lab-data owner embedded with R&D. First deliverable: the schema and a frozen dataset per round, not a model. The assay owner holds the veto on plate validity.

Why believe a Bayesian method helps at all?

It has not beaten the plain average yet, and the memo says so. Its value today is structure: an explicit uncertainty rule, an anchor and pass criteria set in advance. It earns trust when forward rounds beat the average.
